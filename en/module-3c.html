The fundamental law

“You need body language, a tone of voice and eye contact that say: whether you’re interested or not doesn’t bother me.”

— Michael J. Durkin, Double Your Contacts

Before the first word, your prospect reads your posture. If you need them to say yes, they feel it — and instinctively take back control. A successful invitation starts with an inner state: you are selecting, not begging.

F.E.A.R. — The fear that blocks action
FFalse
False evidence — you imagine disasters that haven’t happened yet
EEvidence
Presented as real — your brain treats it as an established fact
AAppearing
Appearing true — it paralyses you before you’ve even dialled the number
RReal
The only antidote: massive action. Every call you make reduces the fear of the next one
The 6 pillars of the right mindset
🎁
An objection = a gift
It proves your invitation has sparked something. A silent prospect wasn’t concerned. The one who objects is present and thinking.
😌
Zero pressure
You aren’t trying to convince everyone — only the truly motivated people. The others are just waiting for the right moment.
🚫
Toxic words
Drop: “MLM”, “pyramid”, “financial freedom”, “opportunity”. Say instead: “project”, “concept”, “something to show you”.
🚶
The physiology of confidence
Make your calls standing up, walking. Movement frees your voice and projects a natural charisma the other person can feel.
🚪
The exit door
“I don’t know if it’s for you” reduces the prospect’s pressure AND protects your ego if the answer is no. Both sides win.
🪞
Genuine empathy
You had the same fears as they do. Say so. That sincerity creates an immediate connection that opens every door.
The “exit door” technique
🚪 Selling without resistance — The NRS strategy
Most prospects don’t turn down a meeting because they aren’t interested — they turn it down because they are afraid of having to say no to you at the end. By giving them permission to refuse from the start, you remove their biggest fear.

Anti-resistance script: “If after talking about it, it isn’t for you, will you tell me? Can you say no to me?”

By asking permission for a “no”, you pleasantly surprise the prospect, their defences come down and the meeting becomes possible without friction.
The life-support metaphor
🏥 Why act immediately — Rob Sperry
A new partner is like someone on life support. Their level of conviction is high, but their emotional resilience is at its lowest. A single negative remark from someone close can be enough to stop everything.

Inviting isn’t only about finding customers — it’s about generating immediate proof of concept for yourself. The first win (a customer, a partner) gives your start emotional security.

The antidote to fear: massive, immediate action. You don’t need to know everything before inviting. The scripts below are designed to be used right now, with no prior expertise.
Where to start — Your 5 Favourites
Before working your whole list: identify your 5 favourite people — the ones you have the most affinity and trust with. Getting a first win among these 5 people validates the model and gives you the momentum to keep going. Start with them.
Adapting your invitation to the 4 profiles

A good inviter doesn’t say the same thing to everyone. They observe, identify the person’s profile, and adapt their message to what really motivates them. These scripts are starting points — rephrase them in your own words.

🔴 Red Profile — Leader
Dominant, formal, likes to lead, decides fast. Motivated by money, competition and success. Respect their time — get straight to the point.
→ Direct business approach, short. Talk business, income, profitability. Value their leadership.
🔵 Blue Profile — Social
Very sociable, loves relationships, parties, adventure and being in the spotlight. Gets bored quickly with too much detail. Motivated by fun and human connections.
→ Highlight the community, events, travel, the team spirit and meeting people.
🟢 Green Profile — Analytical
Structured, formal, asks lots of questions, needs facts and details. Slow to decide but very loyal once convinced. Don’t rush them.
→ BalanceTest, clinical studies, scientific data, stock market listing. Give them time to analyse.
🟡 Yellow Profile — Caring
Very empathetic, loyal, deeply loves helping others. Health, family and security are their priorities. Avoids conflict.
→ Highlight the mission of helping, the support, helping those around them regain their health and making a positive difference.

The 5 C rule — the framework for every invitation

The only goal of the invitation is to get a meeting. Never present or sell the concept over the phone or by message. The 5 Cs are the pillars of every successful invitation.

C
Concise
2 to 3 sentences maximum. The longer it is, the more you invite premature questions and objections. Brevity creates curiosity.
C
Curious
Make them want to know more without saying everything. The goal isn’t to inform — it’s to create enough curiosity to get a yes to the meeting.
C
Confident
Calm voice, steady pace, standing and walking. Your inner certainty comes across better than any argument. People feel it before the words.
C
Compliment
Give a sincere, authentic compliment on the person’s attitude, professionalism or a quality you have really observed. Never made up.
C
Confirm
Always offer two precise date options : “Thursday at 2 pm or Friday at 10 am?” A vague appointment doesn’t exist. Two dates = far more confirmations.
The Zinzino 5-step structure (Express Start)
📋 Structure of the phone invitation
1. Opening — “Hello [First name], it’s [Your Name]. How are you?”

2. Agree on the time and place FIRST — “When are you free this week? Monday or Wednesday?” (Setting the time before explaining why avoids premature questions)

3. Explain the reason — “The reason I’d like to meet you is…” (see scripts)

4. Edify the person coming with you — “As it’s still new to me, I’ll come with a colleague who has more experience with the products.”

5. Confirm — “See you Monday at 8 pm at [Place]. Does that work for you?”
The visual support shield: If the prospect asks “What is it?”, never go into detail. Answer: “It’s in test-based nutrition, I’ll explain everything when we meet — I need something visual so it’s clear.”

The magic transition sentence — “If I… will you…”
🔑 Getting a psychological commitment from the prospect
As soon as the prospect shows interest, get a precise commitment with this structure:

“If I send you the video link, will you watch it tonight?”
“If I invite you to a webinar on Thursday evening, will you join?”
“If I call you back on Wednesday morning, will you have seen it?”

This structure commits the person to keeping their side of the deal. A “yes” to this question is worth far more than a vague “OK”.

The 11 traps to avoid at all costs
Presenting the concept on the phone or by message — the invitation is only for getting a meeting
Being too enthusiastic — excitement drives people away, calm attracts them
Talking for too long — 2 to 3 sentences maximum, no more
Answering every question before the presentation — keep the curiosity intact
Using the words “MLM”, “network marketing”, “pyramid” or “financial freedom” when inviting
Doing the invitation and the presentation at the same time — always two separate steps
Not confirming the meeting — a call the day before multiplies the attendance rate
Forgetting to offer two dates — “Thursday or Friday?” gets far more yeses than a single date
Talking about the business before creating a real human connection
Accepting an “I’ll think about it” without setting a precise call-back date
Believing your prospects will get back to you — you are always the one who follows up

Your daily invitation routine
📅 The daily sequence — iron rule: at least 3 invitations a day
Morning (15 min) — Identify 3 contacts to invite. Mentally prepare the approach suited to their profile.

Midday (30 min) — Make the calls or send the texts. Always standing, walking.

Evening (15 min) — Follow up: who replied, who to chase, which meetings to confirm. Update the CRM.

Weekly goal: 15 contacts a week = 60 a month.
Practice — the key to everything
You don’t learn to invite by reading — you learn by doing. These scripts are starting points. Practise with your sponsor in role plays, practise out loud, find the words that sound like you. Being clumsy at first is normal and temporary. That’s what builds the skill.
🎭
Role play with your sponsor
At least twice a week. Your sponsor plays the difficult prospect. You learn to stay calm and composed in the face of objections and silences.
🎙️
Practising out loud
Read the scripts standing up, record yourself, listen. Correct your tone, pace and silences. Your voice is your number one tool.
📓
Invitation log
Write down every invitation: who, when, the answer, the next step. Analyse your numbers to find where you can improve.
🔄
Post-call debrief
After every difficult call, ring your sponsor within 10 minutes. Don’t let doubt settle in on your own. This is a team sport.
