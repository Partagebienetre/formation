The Edification

Edification is the art of transferring your trust to an expert before they speak. One well-edified three-way call is worth ten presentations on your own.

What is edification?

“You can present a product online. But you connect hearts when you are with people.”

— Oslo Convention 2025

Edification is the process by which you introduce an expert or your sponsor to your prospect so as to create perceived authority before they have even opened their mouth. Without edification, your prospect arrives neutral. With good edification, they arrive ready to listen and to trust.

The key principle: In network marketing, you are the link of trust. Your role isn’t to know everything — it’s to connect the right people. Edification makes that connection work.
The Triangle of Trust

Understand the transfer of credibility before using the scripts

🙋YouTrust
→
👤ProspectCuriosity
→
⭐ExpertAuthority

The prospect trusts you. When you edify the expert, that trust transfers to them. The prospect then listens to the expert with a level of attention they would never have had without that introduction. It’s relationship physics.

The 5 principles of good edification
1
Be sincere
Don’t make things up. Highlight what you truly admire about this expert — their journey, their results, the way they support people. Sincerity shows, and so does exaggeration.
2
Create scarcity
“They are in high demand”, “I’ll try to get them”, “they might agree”. These words position the expert as someone of value, not as someone waiting for your call.
3
Edify BEFORE the meeting
Do the edification on the phone or face to face, always before the expert arrives or before connecting them. An expert who edifies themselves loses all credibility.
4
Hand over and stay quiet
Once the introduction is made, your role is over. Let the expert talk. Don’t interrupt, don’t finish their sentences. Your silence is your biggest contribution.
5
Edify your team too
Edification isn’t only for three-way calls. Edify your partners in front of their contacts. Edify events and speakers. Everything you value gains power.
Edification scripts — Tap to open
A
Edification before the three-way call
What to say to the prospect before connecting them
▼
📞 What you say to the prospect BEFORE
“[First name], you asked excellent questions. I want to make sure you get the most accurate answers.

I’ll try to see if I can get 10 minutes with my senior partner, [Expert’s first name]. They have remarkable success — they build teams internationally. They are in high demand, but since I’ve told them about you, they might agree to give us a moment.

Be brief and precise, because their time is precious.”
⚡ Why it works“Try to get them” + “in high demand” = the prospect arrives actively listening, not on the defensive.
B
The introduction during the three-way call
Handing over — then total silence
▼
🔄 Once everyone is connected
“Hello [Expert], thank you for taking the time. Let me introduce [Prospect], the person I told you about — [job or quick context].

[Prospect], let me introduce [Expert], one of the most respected leaders in our network, who supports me and teaches me a great deal.”
🤫 After the introductionStay completely silent. Only speak again if the expert explicitly invites you to. Your silence is a sign of respect and strengthens the expert’s authority.
C
Face-to-face edification
When the sponsor arrives after the prospect
▼
🏠 Before the sponsor arrives (coffee, home meeting)
“Oh, I told you I’d try to get [First name] to come tonight. Good news, they managed to make it! They’ve been doing this for [length of time] and have really solid results. I trust them completely. They’ll explain it much better than me.”
🎯 The techniqueHave your sponsor arrive AFTER your prospect. That way, the prospect sees you welcoming someone important — not someone who was already waiting there.
D
Edifying an event or Zoom
To make people want to come
▼
📅 When you invite someone to a Zoom or a meeting
“There’s an online presentation on Tuesday evening. The speakers have been doing this for years, it’s professional and concrete — not salesy at all. It’s 45 minutes. I’ll send you the link, keep the slot free.”
💡 RuleAlways edify the event before sending the link. A bare link with no context is 3 times less likely to lead to someone attending.
Mistakes to avoid at all costs
Edifying yourself in front of the prospect (“I’m very successful in this field…”)
Exaggerating the expert’s results so much that the prospect is disappointed when meeting them
Talking while the expert presents — it breaks their authority and your credibility
Forgetting to edify before the call and trying to do it at the last second in front of the expert
Not edifying at all and launching into an improvised presentation on your own

— Fundamental principle of network marketing
